# Convergence Constitution v0.2

1. EC may learn. EVO and Eidos must not learn at runtime.
2. EC may propose change. EVO/Eidos accept only versioned, validated and authorized change.
3. EC knowledge is never automatically EVO business truth.
4. EVO remains execution authority for enterprise Commands and facts.
5. Eidos remains deterministic experience validation/realization and must not become a competing long-term brain.
6. Cross-system integration uses public, versioned contracts; no repository imports another repository's private internals.
7. No cross-system adapter may write directly to another system's database.
8. Human/authorized policy approval remains explicit for high-impact decisions.
9. Learning from outcomes creates candidate knowledge; it does not silently promote universal truth.
10. Package installation and knowledge merge must preserve provenance, version, compatibility and auditability.
11. EC and the LLM(s) operating with EC act as enterprise-domain experts across business and technology roles as needed, such as CEO/management advisor, CIO, CTO, functional business expert, implementation consultant, IT operations expert and other specialist roles. These are advisory/implementation/operations intelligence roles, not sources of EVO operational truth or substitutes for enterprise authorization.
12. During initial enterprise-system establishment, EC + its LLM(s) primarily act as system implementation experts: understand the enterprise, consume EVO/Eidos public contracts, compile/propose enterprise packages and experience intent, assist validation, configuration, migration and go-live, and diagnose gaps without writing private internals of EVO/Eidos.
13. After go-live, EC + its LLM(s) primarily act as ongoing operations, management and improvement experts: observe governed enterprise information, diagnose, recommend, research, maintain knowledge, propose governed changes and assist IT/operational maintenance. Their participation may improve the enterprise system but is not required for normal deterministic execution.
14. EVO + Eidos together must remain a fully operable enterprise system when EC and all LLMs are absent or unavailable. Published enterprise definitions, authorized Commands, deterministic business execution, governed facts and deterministic human experience must continue to operate normally without EC inference.
15. EVO's project/development LLM and Eidos's project/development LLM are engineering actors used to design, implement, test and maintain their respective products; they are not runtime participants in a customer's enterprise business process. Runtime architecture must not depend on those project-owner LLMs being present.
16. EC intelligence must degrade safely: loss/unavailability of EC or its LLM may remove advisory, learning, diagnosis, recommendation, research or improvement capabilities, but must not corrupt, block or invalidate the already-published deterministic EVO + Eidos enterprise runtime unless an explicitly governed enterprise policy defines a particular optional dependency.
