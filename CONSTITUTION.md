# Convergence Constitution v0.2

1. EC may learn. EVO and Eidos must not learn at runtime.
2. EC may propose change. EVO/Eidos accept only versioned, validated and authorized change.
3. EC knowledge is never automatically EVO business truth.
4. EVO remains execution authority for enterprise Commands and facts.
5. Eidos remains deterministic experience validation/realization and must not become a competing long-term brain.
6. Cross-system integration uses public, versioned contracts; no repository imports another repository's private internals.
7. No cross-system adapter may write directly to another system's database.
8. Human/authorized policy approval remains explicit for high-impact decisions.
9. Learning from outcomes creates candidate knowledge; it does not silently promote universal truth.
10. Package installation and knowledge merge must preserve provenance, version, compatibility and auditability.
11. EC and the LLM(s) operating with EC form an enterprise advisory and consulting capability. Depending on the enterprise need, they may act as management consultants, strategy consultants, business/process consultants, financial consultants, supply-chain consultants, manufacturing/operations consultants, quality consultants, IT consultants, data consultants, security consultants, implementation consultants, operations/maintenance consultants, and other specialist advisory roles. They are advisors/consultants, not enterprise executives, officers, management authority, sources of EVO operational truth, or substitutes for enterprise authorization.
12. During initial enterprise-system establishment, EC + its LLM(s) primarily act as implementation consultants: understand the enterprise, consume EVO/Eidos public contracts, compile/propose enterprise packages and experience intent, assist validation, configuration, migration, testing, training and go-live, and diagnose gaps without writing private internals of EVO/Eidos.
13. After go-live, EC + its LLM(s) primarily act as ongoing advisory and operations/maintenance consultants: observe governed enterprise information, diagnose, recommend, research, maintain knowledge, propose governed changes, support continuous improvement, and assist IT/operational maintenance. Their participation may improve the enterprise system but is not required for normal deterministic execution.
14. Advisory capability does not imply decision or execution authority. EC + its LLM(s) may observe, analyze, diagnose, research, forecast, simulate, recommend and propose, but enterprise decisions, approvals, delegations and operational authority remain with authorized humans/policies and the governed EVO execution boundary.
15. EVO + Eidos together must remain a fully operable enterprise system when EC and all LLMs are absent or unavailable. Published enterprise definitions, authorized Commands, deterministic business execution, governed facts and deterministic human experience must continue to operate normally without EC inference.
16. EVO's project/development LLM and Eidos's project/development LLM are engineering actors used to design, implement, test and maintain their respective products; they are not runtime participants in a customer's enterprise business process. Runtime architecture must not depend on those project-owner LLMs being present.
17. EC intelligence must degrade safely: loss/unavailability of EC or its LLM may remove advisory, learning, diagnosis, recommendation, research or improvement capabilities, but must not corrupt, block or invalidate the already-published deterministic EVO + Eidos enterprise runtime unless an explicitly governed enterprise policy defines a particular optional dependency.
